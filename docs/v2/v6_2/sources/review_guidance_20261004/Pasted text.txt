4. **Add the supported June/August time sensitivity after reviewing that packet.**

   Keep all eleven Phoenix passes in the descriptive plot. For the sensitivity, calculate:

   \[
   C_{\mathrm{afternoon}}
   =0.50C_{\mathrm{June\,11}}+0.50C_{\mathrm{August\,11}}
   \]

   \[
   C_{\mathrm{morning}}
   =0.50C_{\mathrm{June\,26}}
   +0.25C_{\mathrm{August\,6}}
   +0.25C_{\mathrm{August\,23}}
   \]

   Report afternoon minus morning, plus the June and August comparisons separately. This compares **09:30–11:30 with 15:00–18:00**, rather than estimating an exact 10:30-versus-16:00 difference.

Calibrate temporal uncertainty using the actual sampling design, accounting for repeated geography and avoiding counting first-stage uncertainty twice.

5. **Implement his specific quadratic sensitivity.**

   Our September 30 spline graph is an exploratory addition. **It does not complete his requested simple quadratic check**, particularly because its cities use different references.

   Fit the same cells, blocks and controls with canopy terms \(f\) and \(f^2\). Square raw canopy first, then centre each term within blocks.

   For supported endpoints \(f_1=f_0+0.10\), calculate:

   \[
   \text{Cooling}
   =-\left[\beta_1(f_1-f_0)+\beta_2(f_1^2-f_0^2)\right].
   \]

   Both terms must change in every prediction and bootstrap draw. The required verification example must return **0.44 K cooling** from 10% to 20% canopy.

   Before comparing cities, check observations near both endpoints and overlap in the other controls. The old **2.85%–12.85%** pair needs this stronger support audit; block minima and maxima alone are insufficient.
