# Decisions after the released pilot review

The previously deferred numerical and visual review is complete for the current 2023 pilot. The new evidence makes population and model sensitivity central to the interpretation: the selected-date Phoenix contrast is −0.325 K/+10pp on original populations, −0.106 on the common footprint and −0.433 with the quadratic model. All three fixed-date spatial intervals exclude zero, but these are different analysis populations/forms rather than interchangeable estimates of a universal quantity.

## Decisions recorded now

- Keep temperature primary, all sixteen original pass populations and all six controls. Keep the quadratic and common-footprint analyses as sensitivities; do not select a preferred model/population because of its sign or interval.
- Report the negative Phoenix contrast only for the frozen June/August dates and weights, with the footprint/model sensitivity beside it. Do not issue a general summer or causal diurnal claim.
- Report the temperature–energy discrepancy numerically. Leave practical agreement/equivalence undecided; the numerical tolerance remains unset and any later margin is post-disclosure.
- Preserve rare common-footprint rank failures and all source artifacts. Neither missing draws nor unsupported date deletions are replaced.

## Next scientific priorities

1. **Define the population the research question intends to represent.** The common footprint excludes much of the original geography and changes the estimated magnitude. Any future population definition should be justified by that question and observable support, not by obtaining a stronger or cleaner result. Formal neighborhood-driver attribution has not been performed.
2. **Investigate alignment and composition mechanisms.** Controlled one-cell shifts change pass estimates appreciably, but do not prove an actual registration error. A separately frozen audit of actual geolocation/registration and represented context would clarify the mechanism. Avoid another large thermal expansion before specifying this work.
3. **Plan additional independent dates for the intended time target.** June morning/afternoon and August afternoon each have one contributing Phoenix date. Atlanta's defined windows also have sparse month/arm coverage. Specify the target, practically meaningful difference and acceptable uncertainty before deriving sample requirements. A new numerical minimum pass count has not been invented here.
4. **Reassess the sampling design with metadata before acquisition.** The existing 2023 Phoenix catalogue audit found no July/September morning opportunity in the defined window. Other years require an explicit scope and cannot silently repair the 2023 pilot. Additional cities should supply a documented missing condition, not merely increase counts.

This is a review decision and proposal, not an acquisition order or external communication. The current bounded pilot report can be discussed now. Further data processing, new study populations and sending material to Prof. Alizadeh remain separate actions.
