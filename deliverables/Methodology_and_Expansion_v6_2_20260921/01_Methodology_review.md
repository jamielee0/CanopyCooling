# Methodology review and recommendations — v6.2

21 September 2026. Targeted primary-source review and implementation audit. This is an exploratory amendment within v6.2. It preserves the original pilot and distinguishes evidence, demonstrations and proposed methods.

## Recommended analysis

Keep the paired Stage 1 block-intercept models as the transparent baseline. They answer the intended question: the conditional association of canopy fraction with mixed-pixel surface temperature in one city/pass. Add a limited nonlinear canopy sensitivity and stronger support diagnostics before interpreting differences between cities as physical differences. Continue to report K per +10 percentage points and matching W/m² coefficients, with a consistent sign convention.

After inspecting the pass-level scatterplots, compare a simple time relationship with a low-complexity smooth only when independent days and season coverage support it. A variance-aware hierarchical regression of pass estimates is the strongest candidate for the eventual multi-city analysis: separate first-stage measurement variance from residual between-pass variation, allow city-specific time patterns with partial pooling, and assess predictions by leaving out days/seasons/cities as appropriate. This is a recommendation for validation, not a claim that a more complex model is automatically better or a change already made to the primary estimator.

The total observation-time pattern stays primary and includes the associated change in solar geometry. The secondary pattern standardizes geometry and season only where their joint support exists. Do not interpret a cross-date sequence as repeated observations of the same day's cooling curve.

## Issues found and concrete responses

| Issue | What the audit establishes | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Time/season sampling | Eleven Phoenix passes still cover time unevenly across June–September; Atlanta has five. The expanded scatterplot is available and no new empirical time model has been fitted. | Build month-by-time/day counts, then cover missing combinations across additional study years. Use support-based simulation to determine precision; do not replace five with another arbitrary pass-count gate. |
| Time-model uncertainty | The inherited `pilot_scale.time_contrast` resamples noisy observed pass estimates and additionally jitters them by Stage 1 draws. A controlled simulation found overlarge uncertainty; with measurement noise alone, RMS estimated SE was 0.0728 versus empirical sampling SD 0.0442. | Treat the earlier sealed time-comparison uncertainty as provisional. A tested day-cluster benchmark without extra jitter is implemented, but not fitted to the empirical extension. Validate the final hierarchical/cluster model under temporal and spatial dependence before claims. |
| Canopy range and nonlinearity | Phoenix and Atlanta fit slopes across very different canopy ranges. A shared pair of prediction endpoints does not remove this difference when fitting one global linear slope. | Add a small spline or quadratic sensitivity, compute differences at the same supported canopy values, and inspect the joint support with context variables. The controlled canopy-range demonstration uses identical curved responses and no empirical thermal outcomes. It is not an estimate of actual bias. |
| Spatial dependence | Original residual checks show dependence across 1 km boundaries. Larger resampling groups increase SE. For all eleven Phoenix passes, 8 km SEs are 0.0332–0.0815 K per +10 pp. | Show 1/2/4/8 km sensitivity rather than selecting the smallest SE. Before final inference, examine residual correlation distance and spatial-pattern stability. Eight kilometres is a stress check, not a proven independence distance. |
| Repeated geography across passes | The same ground areas and canopy measurement errors recur over time. Independently perturbing each pass ignores covariance. | Evaluate joint spatial resampling across passes, combined with a coherent temporal or hierarchical model. Avoid stacking several independent sources of bootstrap noise without a calibrated generative model. |
| Changing pixel composition | Clouds and coverage change which neighbourhoods contribute. An intersection over every pass can collapse as the archive grows. | Compare canopy/context distributions by pass, report exclusions, and evaluate a fixed supported reference distribution or stratified composition sensitivity. Do not require an all-years exact-cell intersection. New-pass registration/composition stress tests are still pending. |
| Solar/view geometry | The 25° pilot exception is wider than the intended 15° primary geometry set, and view azimuth is incomplete. Only three of the eleven Phoenix candidates meet 15°. | Retain the documented exception for this exploratory extension; report geometry sensitivity. Do not claim that a geometry-standardized 10:30-to-16:00 contrast is estimable merely because the total-time endpoints are observed. |
| Geometry-standardization feasibility | Time, day of year, latitude and solar elevation are physically linked. Earlier/later groups having overlapping convex hulls is only a necessary screening check. | Check support at the actual prediction targets and allowed reference settings. Holding time, season and solar elevation at incompatible combinations gives extrapolation. If necessary, report a season-matched pattern retaining actual geometry and a separate descriptive geometry analysis. |
| Data vintage and selection | The model uses stable 2019–2025 median canopy, 2021 imperviousness and 2024 land cover for 2023 passes. Stability selection defines a restricted population. | Complete the annual matched-canopy and context-vintage sensitivities. Describe the target as stable-canopy retained urban cells. A brief high-resolution check remains validation, not a replacement multi-city product. |
| Measurement and physical interpretation | M=εWBσT⁴ is broadband emitted exitance derived from the same retrieved temperature/emissivity pair; it is not an independent radiance observation or net radiation. L2T is a distributed 70 m product already gridded by NASA. | Keep paired cells/draws and exact reference back-transformation. Use “native distributed product grid; no additional LST interpolation.” Audit QC subfields/LST-error information as a sensitivity; do not confuse small regression SE with pixel-level absolute accuracy. |
| Causal interpretation | Within-block controls do not identify a planting intervention or disentangle irrigation, background material, shade, morphology and species. Canopy endpoints supported marginally may still lack joint context support. | Continue using “canopy-associated surface cooling.” Do not translate gradients into air-temperature, human-comfort, transpiration or causal VPD claims. |
| Prior disclosure and tolerance | Original gradients were disclosed at the user's request before Reza supplied the numerical scale-agreement tolerance. | Label the extension exploratory. Reza can set a scientifically meaningful tolerance for subsequent work, but it cannot retrospectively be described as blinded to all effects. Consider an untouched future-year/pass subset for confirmatory evaluation. |

These findings do not invalidate the completed Stage 1 estimates. They limit the strength of the time-pattern, cross-city and physical interpretations. The inherited time-uncertainty routine is the specific implementation issue demonstrated here.

## Primary-source reading and implications

1. **Diurnal evolution of urban tree temperature at a city scale (2021), Scientific Reports.** Twelve clear-sky images form a pseudo-diurnal sequence. The paper estimates component temperatures through constrained local surface-mixture modelling and then studies their relationships. The methods and caveats distinguish this from our mixed-pixel canopy gradient and explicitly acknowledge observations from different days. Keep the physical motivation, not the eight-class unmixing implementation. [Paper and methods](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-89972-0).

2. **Zhao et al. (2023), Satellite-based evidence highlights a considerable increase of urban tree cooling benefits from 2000 to 2015.** The 806-city study fits power curves to mean LST in 1%-wide canopy bins, takes their local slopes at chosen canopy levels, and standardizes those slopes to a reference air temperature before examining drivers with boosted regression trees. It supports attention to canopy level and baseline conditions when comparing cities. Its VPD finding is an observational climatic association. It does not override our own poor identification/support for a VPD claim. [Global Change Biology](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/gcb.16667). Review scope: publisher abstract and Section 2.4 of the [author-hosted methods](https://srs.bnu.edu.cn/docs/2023-11/7be80c6ae4af4a998114dc809c5b8546.pdf). The binned citywide power-curve method does not supply our within-block context adjustment.

3. **Li et al. (2024), Green spaces provide substantial but unequal urban cooling globally.** This study uses adjusted LST–NDVI regressions across urban agglomerations. It supplies precedent for a transparent regression-based cooling-efficiency metric, while NDVI and tree-canopy fraction remain different predictors. Local nonlinearities and the handling of coefficient signs matter for comparisons. Preserve signed canopy gradients and test nonlinear sensitivity rather than inheriting every processing choice. [Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-51355-0).

4. **Hourly impact of urban forests on land surface temperature based on machine learning (2025).** The Rome study uses twelve ECOSTRESS summer scenes, random forests, SHAP and partial dependence to explore nonlinear time-varying relationships with tree structure. This motivates a nonlinear diagnostic and confirms that diurnal vegetation–LST analysis already exists. Predictive fit/SHAP is not a substitute for an identified conditional contrast. [Results in Engineering](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2590123025036370). Review scope: publisher abstract/highlights; do not claim to have audited its full validation design.

5. **Zhang et al. (2025), Global evaluation of high-resolution ECOSTRESS land surface temperature and emissivity products: Collection 1 versus Collection 2.** The validation reports changes in temperature bias across collections and temperature ranges. Keep collection/build identity explicit and do not interpret unmatched retrieval changes as city/time effects. The published validation is not a pass-specific accuracy guarantee for our urban gradients. [Remote Sensing of Environment](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0034425725002032). Review scope: publisher abstract/highlights; an author-hosted article was located but its full text could not be fetched.

6. **Lewis & Linzer (2005), Estimating Regression Models in Which the Dependent Variable Is Based on Estimates.** Treating estimated coefficients as Stage 2 responses requires separating their sampling variance from other residual variation. The paper cautions against naïve precision weighting and discusses robust and feasible-GLS alternatives. This informs the recommendation to compare a robust day-cluster benchmark with a variance-aware hierarchical model. The bootstrap defect reported above was established by our own code audit/simulation. [Political Analysis](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/political-analysis/article/abs/estimating-regression-models-in-which-the-dependent-variable-is-based-on-estimates/BE545AD531D482F5D052A27662C37FA7).

7. **Roberts et al. (2017), Cross-validation strategies for data with temporal, spatial, hierarchical, or phylogenetic structure.** Random folds can overstate generalization when nearby observations are dependent. Validation must reflect the intended new days, areas or cities, and blocking can itself force extrapolation. Use this guidance to evaluate a flexible model, not as proof that an arbitrary block size yields valid coefficient intervals. [Ecography](https://nsojournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/ecog.02881).

8. **NASA/JPL ECOSTRESS product documentation.** Distinguishes swath, gridded and tiled products and provides layer/QC definitions. Keep consistent collections and matching emissivity; describe the product grid accurately. [Official product guides](https://ecostress.jpl.nasa.gov/data/atbds-summary-table), [Collection 2 LST/emissivity user guide](https://lpdaac.usgs.gov/documents/1574/ECOL2_User_Guide_V2.pdf).

All links were checked during this targeted review on 21 September 2026; some publisher full-text endpoints were unavailable. This targeted review does not establish exhaustive novelty coverage. A defensible proposed contribution is a supported multi-city assessment of fixed-overpass representativeness, with matched temperature/emitted-energy analyses and explicit precision/scale checks. Do not claim to be the first study of diurnal urban tree cooling.

## Decisions to take to Reza

- Recommend retaining the pooled linear model as baseline, adding a common-support nonlinear sensitivity, and validating a variance-aware Stage 2 model. Do not switch the primary model because one method produces a preferred city difference.
- Request a substantive scale-agreement tolerance, distinguish it from the 0.10 K SE benchmark, and acknowledge the prior disclosure. A held-out evaluation can recover a prospective test for later data.
- Decide whether the final inference uses the intended 15° set or a justified broader geometry design after examining nonthermal coverage. Changing angle limits changes the sample; more passes do not resolve that decision by themselves.
- Choose the final 8–12 additional cities after extending the canopy/cloud screen across the study years and checking morning support and geometry. The present shortlist is a concrete starting point, not a frozen national sample.
