# Methods and figure captions — released pilot review

## Methods

We analyzed eleven Phoenix and five Atlanta ECOSTRESS passes from 2023 using the frozen paired native-cell samples. Each city-pass model estimated one canopy association with 1 km block intercepts and six contextual controls. Temperature was primary; emitted longwave flux and its fixed-reference temperature equivalent were paired diagnostics. Native observations, controls and resampling were matched across outcomes. The primary linear specification, QA, references and all original sample populations were preserved.

The original Phoenix time sensitivity compared the specified morning and afternoon windows in June and August, with equal month weights in each arm. Spatial uncertainty used whole physical groups shared across dates; structurally absent groups contributed zero sufficient statistics. Three date deletions emptied a required month/arm and were explicitly unsupported. Two valid deletions retained the frozen month/arm target. No spatial draw or pixel was counted as an independent temporal observation.

The common-footprint analysis retained the exact 196,090-cell intersection across all eleven Phoenix passes and was treated as a population sensitivity. The quadratic model added raw canopy squared before within-block centering, with both canopy terms protected and all six controls retained. Finite contrasts changed both canopy terms. The historical endpoint pair remained fixed; other candidate endpoints were reported without outcome-based reselection.

After effect disclosure on 6 October 2026, a focused exploratory follow-up computed joint window uncertainty across the original linear, common-footprint linear and original-population quadratic variants. All fifteen archived pass-level points were reconstructed before generating a prospectively recorded new draw sequence. The reference-centered quadratic basis was algebraically identical to the earlier raw-square model; no new functional form was selected. Four of 1,000 coarse-group draws omitted the common sample's bare-cover information and were retained as non-estimable, without control removal or replacement. This follow-up was not blinded and is labeled post-disclosure.

For each time-comparison arm, flux changes were averaged before exact fixed-reference conversion; arm equivalents were then differenced. The temperature reference and emissivity were held fixed. Scale discrepancies were reported with matched uncertainty, without declaring practical agreement or promoting energy to the primary outcome.

The preceding synthetic calibration used actual schedules and estimated spatial covariance under declared hypothetical mean/day processes. Its imperfect coverage and sparse independent-date support did not justify a general-summer interval. All intervals in this released empirical report are conditional spatial intervals for the specified samples/dates, and the multiple exploratory comparisons are not multiplicity-adjusted.

## Captions

**Phoenix time comparison.** Original-population June, August, combined and supported deletion contrasts appear on the left with Step7 8 km spatial intervals. The right panel shows the explicitly post-disclosure, matched original/common/quadratic follow-up. Negative values indicate weaker canopy-associated cooling on the sampled afternoon dates. The direction persists in these checks, but the population/model changes alter its magnitude. Dates are fixed; no same-day causal or general-summer interpretation follows.

**Common footprint.** Original and common-population cooling estimates and their paired changes for eleven Phoenix passes. All common estimates are lower; 8 km percentile intervals for each paired change exclude zero. The common sample retains 35%–57% of each original pass and changes the represented geography/context. It is a sensitivity analysis, not an automatically preferred population.

**Quadratic model.** Quadratic-minus-linear +10pp temperature contrasts at approximately 2.85% and 12.85% canopy. The models share the original samples, controls and bootstrap draws. These are endpoint-specific effects; city support differs, and the curves do not establish a universal response function or model-selection victory.

**Temperature and energy.** Original five-date temperature and fixed-reference energy-equivalent time contrasts, with paired discrepancy intervals. Both give a negative contrast, while their magnitudes differ. The routes use the same retrievals and provide no independent validation. No agreement tolerance or formal scale verdict has been imposed.

**Registration.** Shifted-minus-unshifted cooling on identical cells for 24 one-cell perturbations across the six added Phoenix passes. Intervals are the inherited paired 1 km whole-block intervals. These are deliberate alignment stress tests, not estimates of actual displacement. Available-support shifts are kept separately because both alignment and population can change.
